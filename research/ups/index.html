A Reproducible MCI-Centered Ubiquitin–Proteasome Transcriptional Signature

Peripheral blood gene expression across three Alzheimer's disease datasets

Maya Afergan · Arij Daou   |   Research in progress

Research

Project overview

Research overview

The ubiquitin–proteasome system (UPS) plays a critical role in protein quality control. Distinct regulatory changes in mild cognitive impairment (MCI) may offer insight into disease-associated molecular processes and potential biomarkers. Previous work has observed that some genes exhibit non-monotonic expression patterns across healthy aging, MCI, and Alzheimer's disease (AD), but these deviations have not been extensively examined within a specific biological pathway.

This study investigates whether blood transcriptomic alterations in UPS-related protein degradation exhibit a reproducible, MCI-centered pattern rather than a simple monotonic relationship with cognitive diagnosis. Particular attention is given to reduced expression in the MCI group relative to controls and AD.

We analyzed three public blood gene-expression datasets: GSE63060, GSE63061, and GSE140829. Genes associated with the UPS were evaluated using age- and sex-adjusted measures of MCI-centered deviation. Among non-monotonic genes, effect magnitude was compared with expression-matched non-UPS genes using Monte Carlo simulations with 100,000 iterations.

Across the three datasets, UPS genes exhibited larger MCI-centered deviations than matched non-UPS genes. The signal was driven primarily by MCI-centered reductions, or “valleys,” rather than increases. Signed UPS deviations also showed substantial concordance across cohorts. An analysis of 876 Gene Ontology Biological Process gene sets placed UPS-related effects among the strongest cross-dataset patterns.

These observations motivate additional studies of proteostasis-related transcription in cognitive impairment, including longitudinal validation. The findings concern peripheral blood transcription and should not be interpreted as direct measurements of UPS protein activity in the brain.
