A Reproducible MCI-Centered Ubiquitin-Proteasome Transcriptional Signature in Peripheral Blood Across Alzheimer’s Disease Datasets

Maya Afergan, Arij Daou
Harvard Radcliffe Institute, Harvard University, Cambridge, USA
Accepted for presentation at Neuroscience 2026
Society for Neuroscience annual meeting · Washington, DC
Sunday, November 15 · 11 a.m.–noon ET · WCC Halls A–C · Board A42

UPS-related transcription shows an unusually strong and reproducible MCI-centered pattern across three blood datasets, driven primarily by MCI-centered reductions and ranking among the strongest GO pathway effects.

  1. UPS genes show larger MCI-centered deviations than expression-matched non-UPS genes across two related and one independent dataset.
  2. Valley enrichment exceeded peak enrichment in all three datasets, indicating that the UPS signal is driven primarily by MCI-centered reductions rather than increases.
  3. UPS ranked in the top 5% by cross-dataset effect magnitude. Of 876 GO Biological Process pathways, only 3 were significant in all three datasets, and 2 substantially overlapped with UPS.
Read the abstract

The ubiquitin-proteasome system (UPS) is central to cellular proteostasis, yet it remains unclear whether UPS-related transcriptional changes vary systematically across cognitive diagnostic groups. We tested whether peripheral blood expression of UPS genes exhibits a reproducible non-monotonic pattern across cognitively healthy controls, mild cognitive impairment (MCI), and Alzheimer’s disease (AD).

Three public blood transcriptomic datasets (GSE63060, GSE63061, and GSE140829) were analyzed. Age- and sex-adjusted linear regression models were used to calculate a standardized MCI-centered contrast for each gene, representing MCI expression relative to the control-AD midpoint. Among non-monotonic genes, pathway-level effects were summarized by the median absolute standardized contrast and compared with expression-matched non-UPS genes using 100,000-iteration Monte Carlo null distributions.

UPS genes showed significantly larger MCI-centered deviations than matched non-UPS genes in all three datasets, including the independently generated GSE140829 cohort (UPS/null ratios 1.1007-1.1425; UPS-null differences 0.0194-0.0286; p=0.0005-0.0211). The effect was driven primarily by MCI-centered reductions ("valleys") rather than increases ("peaks"): valley UPS/null ratios ranged from 1.1118 to 1.2021, with significance in GSE63061 and GSE140829 and concordant direction in GSE63060. Signed UPS deviations were also strongly concordant across datasets (pairwise r=0.61-0.71).

To assess pathway specificity, 876 size-filtered Gene Ontology Biological Process gene sets were analyzed using the same framework. After false discovery rate correction, only three comparator pathways remained significant in all three datasets; two overlapped extensively with UPS. By median cross-dataset effect, UPS ranked in the 95.7th percentile for observed/null ratio and the 95.0th percentile for absolute effect difference.

These findings identify a reproducible, MCI-centered peripheral UPS transcriptional signature that is unusually strong relative to the broader GO landscape and motivates longitudinal investigation of proteostasis-related transcription in cognitive impairment.
